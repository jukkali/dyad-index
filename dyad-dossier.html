Evidence index · Psychedelic-assisted therapy for pairs · Reviewed 17 Sept 2026

Two people, one altered state, and a literature far thinner than the retreat brochures suggest.

A maintained, graded index of what research actually shows about psilocybin and related compounds administered to dyads — couples, parent–child pairs, caregiver–patient pairs, co-founders — inside protocolled clinical and retreat settings. Every claim on this page carries the grade of the evidence behind it. Where the evidence is absent, this page says so.

The bottom line, before anything else

  • 01 The only trial evidence for treating a couple as the unit of treatment uses MDMA, not psilocybin — one uncontrolled trial of six couples, plus a small veteran pilot. Both are encouraging. Neither is a randomised controlled trial.
  • 02 Psilocybin’s relational case is indirect and inferential. It rests on individual trials in which connectedness, empathy and relational repair appear as secondary or qualitative outcomes — never as the primary endpoint, and never with the partner in the room.
  • 03 Microdosing for relationship benefit is not supported. The largest placebo-controlled study of microdosing found that improvements in the microdose group were matched by the placebo group. Anything sold on this premise is selling expectancy.

How this index grades

Four tiers, applied consistently

Most writing in this field flattens a phenomenological interview and a controlled trial into the same word: “research shows.” This index refuses that. Every entry is placed on one of four tiers, and the tier travels with the claim wherever it appears.

The claims, graded

What people say these substances do for a relationship — and what stands behind each sentence

Tier A MDMA · n = 6 couples

Treating the couple, rather than the patient, improves both partners at once

Six couples in which one partner had PTSD completed a condensed Cognitive-Behavioral Conjoint Therapy protocol over seven weeks, with two sessions in which both partners received MDMA. Clinician-, patient- and partner-rated PTSD symptoms improved with effect sizes of d = 1.85–3.59; relationship adjustment and happiness improved at d = 0.64–2.79. Five of six patients no longer met PTSD criteria.

This is the strongest dyadic result in the field. It is also uncontrolled, unblinded, and six couples deep. Treat the effect sizes as a reason to run the trial, not as the trial.

Monson et al., 2020 · European Journal of Psychotraumatology · uncontrolled open trial

Tier A MDMA · secondary outcomes

The gains are relational, not just symptomatic

Secondary analysis of the same cohort found improvements in post-traumatic growth, relational support and social intimacy for both partners. Partners reported less behavioural accommodation — the quiet re-arranging of a household around someone’s symptoms — and less conflict. Patients reported improved empathic concern and psychosocial functioning. Gains held across follow-up.

Behavioural accommodation is the mechanism worth noticing: it is a property of the relationship, not of the person, and individual therapy cannot reach it.

Wagner et al., 2021 · Frontiers in Psychiatry

Tier B Psilocybin · n = 20

Psilocybin shifts people from disconnection toward connection

In qualitative follow-up with twenty patients from an open-label trial for treatment-resistant depression, the central reported change was a move from disconnection to connection — to the senses, the self, other people, and the world — and from avoidance of emotion toward acceptance of it. This finding was substantial enough to generate a dedicated instrument, the Watts Connectedness Scale.

Note what this is and is not: patients described feeling more connected to the people in their lives. No partner was assessed, and no relationship was measured.

Watts, Day, Krzanowski, Nutt & Carhart-Harris, 2017 · Journal of Humanistic Psychology

Tier C Psilocybin · n = 32

Psilocybin raises emotional empathy — but not cognitive empathy

In healthy volunteers given 0.215 mg/kg, psilocybin significantly increased emotional empathy — the felt resonance with another’s state — while leaving cognitive empathy, the accurate inference of what another person thinks, unchanged. Hypothetical moral decision-making was unaffected.

This dissociation deserves more weight than it gets in retreat marketing. Feeling more with your partner is not the same as understanding them more accurately, and a session can deliver the first while leaving the second exactly where it was.

Pokorny, Preller et al., 2017 · International Journal of Neuropsychopharmacology

Tier B Psilocybin · n = 13

Relationships are a dominant theme of the experience itself

An interpretative phenomenological analysis of thirteen patients treated for cancer-related anxiety found “relational embeddedness” among its core themes. Participants described forgiveness of others, encounters with parents, children and partners, love and joy arising specifically through human relationship, and a felt need to narrate the experience to the people close to them.

That last point is the practical one: the material surfaces relationally and then wants somewhere relational to land. In a protocolled dyadic setting, it has one.

Belser, Agin-Liebes, Swift, Terrana, Devenot, Friedman, Guss, Bossis & Ross, 2017 · Journal of Humanistic Psychology

Tier D Psilocybin · n = 191

Microdosing improves intimacy, patience and communication

The largest placebo-controlled microdosing study to date used a self-blinding citizen-science design with 191 participants. Every psychological outcome improved over the four-week dosing period — and improved just as much in the placebo group. The small acute differences that did appear were explained by participants correctly guessing their condition.

People do report better weeks when they microdose. The evidence says the reporting is real and the causal story is not. A couple building a shared practice on this is building on expectancy — which has genuine effects, and should be named as what it is.

Szigeti et al., 2021 · eLife · self-blinded, placebo-controlled

Tier D No trial exists

Psilocybin retreats improve couple outcomes

As of this review, no registered clinical trial administers psilocybin to couples as a dyad. Searches of the trial registries return psilocybin studies that involve a partner only as a required escort home or as a collateral informant on mood change — never as a participant.

Couples retreats using psilocybin are operating well ahead of their evidence base. That is not by itself an argument against them; supervised practice has outrun trial data before. It is an argument for saying so plainly in the intake conversation, and for collecting outcomes.

Registry review, September 2026 · ClinicalTrials.gov

Dose regimes compared

Micro and macro are not two strengths of the same intervention

They are different propositions with different evidence, and conflating them is the most common error in couples-facing psychedelic marketing. One has a mechanism and trial support; the other has a following.

Microdosing

Typical: 0.1–0.3 g dried mushroom, or ~1 g truffle
Schedule: every third day, weeks to months
Sub-perceptual by definition
Placebo-controlled support: none

The proposition is ambient: a couple takes a low dose on a shared rhythm and reports more patience, less reactivity, better conversation. The self-blinded evidence says these gains appear identically in people taking nothing.

There is a second, quieter problem for dyads. A shared microdosing ritual is a genuine relational intervention — a recurring, deliberate, jointly-held practice — and rituals like that work. Attributing their effect to the alkaloid rather than the ritual gets the causal story backwards and makes the practice dependent on a substance it may not need.

Tier D — effects not distinguishable from placebo

Macrodosing

Trial range: 20–30 mg psilocybin, or 0.2–0.4 mg/kg
Schedule: 1–2 sessions, 6–8 hours each
Preparation and integration sessions required
Placebo-controlled support: substantial, in individuals

The proposition is episodic: one or two supervised sessions producing a durable shift, with the therapeutic work happening largely in preparation and integration rather than during the dose itself.

This is where the evidence lives — for depression, existential distress and alcohol use disorder, in individuals. The dyadic extension is reasonable, well-theorised, and still an extension. The honest sentence is: this compound reliably does something to a person, and we have not yet measured what it does to a pair.

Tier B — strong in individuals, untested in dyads

The container

What a protocolled dyadic setting actually consists of

Drawn from the published conjoint-therapy protocols. The sequence matters: in every trial that has worked, the dosing session is the shortest and least therapeutic part of the arc.

01

Screening — both partners, separately

2–4 weeks out

Medical and psychiatric screening for each partner independently: personal and family history of psychosis or bipolar I, cardiovascular status, serotonergic medication. Then the dyadic screen that individual protocols do not have — ongoing intimate partner violence, coercion, and whether each partner is consenting freely. Conducted in separate rooms, because that is the only way the answer is worth anything.

02

Preparation — joint and individual

3–6 sessions

Conjoint therapy proper: identifying the accommodation patterns and avoidance cycles the relationship runs on, and agreeing what the session is for. Published protocols place most of the clinical work here. A couple arriving at a dosing session without a shared formulation is arriving without a container.

03

Dosing — both partners, same room

1–2 sessions, 6–8 hours

Two facilitators minimum for a dyad; published trials use a male–female co-therapy pair. Partners are dosed together so the experience is jointly referenceable afterwards. Extended periods of internal, eyes-closed attention alternate with relational contact, on the couple’s initiative rather than the facilitators’.

04

Integration — where the outcome is decided

3–8 sessions, over months

The dyadic advantage and the dyadic risk both concentrate here. Two people who shared the experience can hold each other to what surfaced — or can jointly construct a flattering story about it. Structured integration with a third party in the room is the difference. The veteran pilot’s partial symptom return at follow-up is an argument for extending this phase, not shortening it.

Risks specific to the dyad

What gets worse when you add a second person

Individual psychedelic risk is reasonably well catalogued. These are the risks that only exist because there are two people, and they are systematically under-discussed.

Consent that isn’t free

One partner wants this; the other agrees to save the relationship. That is not consent, and it is the most common presentation. Separate screening interviews exist precisely to catch it, and a reluctant partner is an exclusion, not an obstacle to manage.

Suggestibility with an interested party present

Psychedelics increase suggestibility. In individual therapy the person in the room is a clinician bound by a professional code. In a dyad, the other person in the room has a stake in the content of your insights. A partner’s framing offered during a session can be absorbed as personal revelation.

Asymmetric experience

One partner has a transformative session; the other has a difficult or unremarkable one. The literature on dyadic outcomes assumes rough symmetry and does not describe what to do when a couple leaves with wildly different reference experiences. Protocols should plan for this because it is common.

The afterglow window

Relational warmth in the weeks after a session is real and is not the same as structural change. Couples routinely make consequential decisions — move, conceive, reconcile, commit — inside it. A standing agreement to defer irreversible decisions for a defined period belongs in the preparation phase.

Separation as an outcome

Some couples emerge with a clear shared recognition that the relationship should end. Whether this counts as treatment success is not a question the outcome measures can answer, and a programme that markets only repair is not equipped to support it. It should be named before dosing, not after.

Contraindications that travel

Active IPV, untreated psychosis risk in either partner, or one partner’s acute crisis can each disqualify the pair rather than the person. Dyadic work has a stricter admission profile than individual work, not a looser one — an inversion of how retreat settings usually screen.

Where this can lawfully happen

Jurisdictions, and what each one actually permits

The word “legal” does a great deal of unearned work in this field. Almost nowhere has legalised psilocybin; a handful of places have built narrow supervised or medical channels, and one widely-cited case is a gap in a schedule rather than a permission.

Jurisdiction Status Mechanism, precisely
Netherlands Schedule gap Psilocybin mushrooms were banned in 2008. Psilocybin-containing sclerotia — truffles — were never added to the Opium Act list, so they remain lawful to sell and consume. Guided retreats operate on that unscheduled status, not on a legalisation or a therapeutic framework, and must avoid medical claims. Carrying truffles across a border is an offence.
Oregon, US Supervised Measure 109 created licensed service centres. Product is purchased and consumed on site with a trained facilitator. Non-medical by design: no diagnosis required, and no clinical claim permitted.
Colorado, US Supervised Proposition 122 licensed healing centres and additionally decriminalised personal cultivation, possession and sharing for adults 21+.
New Mexico, US Medical A medical psilocybin access framework established by legislature rather than ballot initiative — the first US state to take that route.
Australia Medical From July 2023, the first country to permit national prescribing: authorised psychiatrists only, for treatment-resistant depression, under the TGA Authorised Prescriber scheme.
Czechia Medical Effective 1 January 2026, reported as the first EU member state to explicitly regulate medical psilocybin — prescribing restricted to trained psychiatrists for defined indications.
Switzerland Compassionate Exceptional-authorisation permits for medical use of psilocybin, MDMA and LSD have been granted for over a decade where alternatives are exhausted. Case-by-case, not a general framework.
Canada Compassionate Access via the Special Access Programme and individual section 56 exemptions, granted case by case.

Legal status in this field changes faster than any page can track. Verify current status with a qualified local practitioner before relying on any row above. Note also that every framework here is written for an individual patient or participant — none contemplates a dyad as the unit of admission.

The research agenda

Open questions this index exists to track

Q1

Does psilocybin work dyadically at all? The entire dyadic evidence base is MDMA. Psilocybin’s profile — more internal, less interpersonally warm, less verbally fluent — may make it a worse conjoint agent, not a better one. Nobody has tested it.

Q2

Dose together or separately? Every dyadic protocol so far doses both partners simultaneously. Sequential dosing, with one partner sober and holding, has never been compared against it.

Q3

Does emotional-without-cognitive empathy help or harm a couple? The Pokorny dissociation predicts increased felt closeness without increased accuracy — which in a conflicted relationship could deepen a shared misunderstanding rather than resolve it.

Q4

How long do relational gains last? The one follow-up signal available — partial symptom return in the veteran pilot — suggests the honeymoon question is real and unanswered.

Q5

Do non-romantic dyads respond the same way? Parent–adult-child, caregiver–patient and co-founder pairs share the structural features that make couples work plausible. Only caregiver dyads have even a published rationale paper.

Q6

What is the base rate of harm? No dyadic study has been powered to detect adverse relational outcomes, and retreat settings are not collecting them systematically. This is the most fixable gap on the list.
